# Legal check

Checked on November 10, 2026, against the code and the App Store listing for version 1.0.

**This is not legal advice.** It is a careful read of the app, the listing and Apple's rules by someone who is not a lawyer. If Unhook ever earns money, adds accounts or a server, or gets a letter from a company or a lawyer, ask a real lawyer.

## The short answer

Nothing here puts Unhook in serious legal danger. The app collects no data, makes no claim to treat or cure anything, and already had good warnings. The check found wording that was stronger or less careful than it should be, and those places are fixed (see below). Three things are left for Bruce to decide, at the end.

## 1. Privacy: fine

| Checked | Result |
|---|---|
| Network code, analytics, ads, third-party code | None in the app or the widgets. The only links that leave the app are the helpline, the support and privacy pages, Apple's pages, and the founder links. |
| App Privacy answer in App Store Connect | "Data Not Collected" is true. |
| Privacy manifest | Matches the code: no tracking, no collected data, UserDefaults only. |

Because nothing leaves the phone, privacy laws such as GDPR, CCPA and the US state health-data laws have very little to apply to. That changes the moment the app gets a server, accounts, analytics or a cloud AI: see "If the app changes".

## 2. Health wording: fixed

A free self-help app is fine. An app that claims to treat a disease, or gives advice that hurts someone, is not. These were changed in this check:

| Was | Now | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The coach answered messages about withdrawal symptoms or medicine with normal craving tips. | A fixed reply: it can't advise, talk to a doctor or pharmacist, and when to call an emergency number. | The coach must not guess about withdrawal or medicine. |
| The doctor warning showed only for Alcohol. | It also shows for "Something else", worded for drugs and daily medicines. | "Something else" could be a pill someone must not stop suddenly. |
| "The first hours are the hardest, and you're already through them." | "The first hours are done. That's a real start." | For a heavy drinker the risky days come later. |
| Alcohol, day 3: only good news. | Adds when to get medical help right away. | Same reason. |
| "Based on guidance from the WHO and the NHS." | "Adapted from public WHO and NHS pages on quitting smoking. Unhook is not connected to or endorsed by them." | The old line could read as if they approved the app. The listing and the review notes say the same now. |
| "Heart risk halved", "Lung cancer risk halved", "A non-smoker's heart" | "…about halved", "Heart risk like a non-smoker's" | The titles were stronger than the sources. The detail lines already said "about". |
| Porn timeline: "less guilt and more self-respect", "they always pass" | Neutral wording | No moral judgment, no promise. |
| Sugar and junk food: nothing about eating. | "…not skipping meals", "…not fewer of them" | Teenagers use the app. |
| "Cravings peak and fade within minutes.", "Low blood sugar makes cravings feel stronger than they are." | "Most cravings…", "Being hungry can make cravings feel stronger." | Stated as fact with no source. |
| Low mood ("depressed", "hopeless") got comfort tips only. | The coach still answers, and the helpline banner shows. | Real help should be one tap away. |
| Crisis reply | Also says "or tell an adult you trust" and "In the US, you can call or text 988". More phrases and misspellings are caught. | |
| The slip sheet had no helpline. | A quiet "Feeling really low? Find a free helpline." link. | A slip is a low moment. |
| The review notes said these habits "appear only as names". | They say each has suggested reasons and a general timeline. | Notes to Apple must be exact. |

What was already good: the "not medical advice" line under every timeline, the helpline in several places, crisis detection in 14 languages before any coach sees a message, the emergency reply, and no use of words like treat, cure, therapy or clinically proven anywhere in the app or the listing.

## 3. The coach: fine, with one line added

Some US states now have rules for chat apps that give emotional support (California, Utah, New York and Illinois are the ones to know). The common points are: say clearly that it is automated, don't call it therapy, and have a plan for people in crisis. Unhook does all three. The coach header now starts "An automated coach, not a person or a doctor." Don't describe the coach as a therapist, a counselor or treatment in any future text.

## 4. The name "Unhook": the one real risk

- About 20 apps on the US App Store have "Unhook" or "Unhooked" in the name, several of them quit-habit apps. The closest is "Unhook: Quit Lust & Reboot" (May 2025). None is big.
- A search of trademark databases found no live US or EU registration for UNHOOK for apps. It found a UK application for UNHOOK filed in October 2026 that covers downloadable apps and coaching; it was not registered yet, and the UK register itself could not be opened to confirm it. A live US mark UNHOOKED belongs to a TV series about addiction recovery.
- What this means: Apple is unlikely to reject the name, and a lawsuit against a free app is very unlikely. The realistic risk is that someone complains to Apple later and Unhook has to be renamed in an update. The name is also hard to find in search among so many lookalikes.
- Cheap protection: always show the full name "Unhook: Quit Bad Habits", never use ™ or ®, never put another app's name in the keywords, keep a backup name ready, and answer any notice from Apple quickly. Search https://tmsearch.uspto.gov for UNHOOK yourself (10 minutes), and look at the UK application again after January 6, 2027.

## 5. Things that are fine

- **Art and fonts.** The sky is drawn by the project's own code. Fredoka and Nunito are under the SIL Open Font License, which allows this use. The photo is Bruce's own.
- **Apple's names.** iPhone, Siri, Face ID and Apple Intelligence are used only to describe what the app works with, and none is in the app's name or icon.
- **"100% free".** True while Unhook Pro stays off.
- **Reviews.** The app only uses Apple's own rating prompt and gives nothing in return for a review.
- **EU trader status.** "Non-trader" fits a free app with no ads.
- **Medical device question.** "No" fits: the app does not diagnose, treat or monitor a disease.

## If the app changes

| If you… | Then… |
|---|---|
| Turn on Unhook Pro or any payment | Update every "free" line, the privacy policy, the paid-apps agreement and tax forms in App Store Connect, and the EU trader status (a trader's address and phone number are shown publicly in the EU). |
| Add a server, accounts, sync, analytics, ads or a cloud AI | Redo the privacy policy and the App Privacy answers first, ask for consent before any coach message leaves the phone, and have a lawyer read the policy. |
| Add a new habit preset or new health text | Keep it hedged ("many people", "often"), keep the doctor line, and never promise a result. |
| Say the app treats addiction, or call the coach therapy | Don't. That is what turns a self-help app into a regulated one. |
